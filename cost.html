Cost saving analysis

A lower bill that still copes when you are busy. Fransystems reads what the Amazon or Microsoft bill is actually buying, and which changes reduce the spend without making the service fragile.

What businesses are already wasting

These are published survey estimates. They describe the market, not a promise for one company. They are the reason a bill is worth reading against the setup that produced it.

  • 27%

    of server and platform spend is estimated wasted, on computers and services that are paid for and not usefully used.

  • 17%

    is how far public cloud spend ran over budget, on average. Managing that spend is the top cloud challenge for 84% of the same respondents.

  • £33 billion

    is the sterling equivalent of $44.5 billion, the 2025 estimate of wasted cloud infrastructure spend worldwide.

Flexera, 2025 State of the Cloud Report, from 759 cloud decision-makers: 27% estimated waste on IaaS and PaaS, and spend over budget by 17%. Harness, FinOps in Focus 2025, estimated that 21% of cloud infrastructure spend would be wasted in 2025, equal to $44.5 billion. At about 75 pence to the dollar on 26 October 2026, that is roughly £33 billion worldwide, not a UK-only total.

The saving on one company is the waste that is sitting idle, too big for the job, or expensive because of how it was built. Spare capacity for a real busy period, and a way to undo a change, should stay. The review separates those from the waste.

What Fransystems does with the bill

The work starts from the whole setup, on Amazon, Microsoft, or both. That means the accounts, the network, the servers and services, the data, and how they connect. It is not a sorted list of the ten largest lines on the invoice. You leave with the waste, the saving, and the change that keeps the service running properly.

What the review covers

  • Servers and services that are bigger than the work they do
  • Things that are unused, forgotten, or left behind by an old project
  • A design that is expensive because of how it is arranged, not because customers are busy
  • Test and spare copies left running at full size, and full price, all month
  • Discounts for paying ahead, looked at only after the waste is visible

What you receive

  • A list of changes in order of priority, not a dump of the billing screen
  • What each change does to how reliably the service stays up
  • Which items are safe to do this week, and which need the design changed first
  • Enough detail for your own team to do it, or for Fransystems to build it in Terraform

Fransystems specialists recently did this for one company. Cloud costs came down by 32%, a saving of more than $140,000.

AWS and Azure, on the same engagement if needed

The cost work comes from running both clouds, not from one supplier’s sales guide. On Amazon that includes several accounts, ordinary servers, and the services that run the applications. On Microsoft it includes their hosted web apps and virtual machines. The questions are the same: what is sitting idle, what is larger than it needs to be, and what a simpler design would stop charging for every month.

Fransystems specialists do this while the service is still being delivered, including while the setup is being written down in Terraform. A saving that depends on someone remembering the same clicks next time is a weak saving. Where the change belongs in the written setup, it can be done that way. The Terraform page describes that work. The architecture page covers the cases where the bill is a symptom of the design.

This is not a race to the smallest possible invoice. The recommendation still has to keep the service reliable. A cheaper design that wakes someone every night is not a saving.
