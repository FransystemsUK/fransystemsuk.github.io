Cost saving analysis

A lower bill that still survives a peak. Fransystems reads what the AWS or Azure bill is buying, and which changes reduce spend without making the platform fragile.

What businesses are already wasting

These are published survey estimates. They describe the market, not a guarantee for one estate. They are the reason a bill is worth reading against the topology that produced it.

  • 27%

    of infrastructure and platform spend is estimated wasted, on compute and services that are paid for and not usefully used.

  • 17%

    is how far public cloud spend ran over budget, on average. Managing that spend is the top cloud challenge for 84% of the same respondents.

  • £33 billion

    is the sterling equivalent of $44.5 billion, the 2025 estimate of wasted cloud infrastructure spend worldwide.

Flexera, 2025 State of the Cloud Report, from 759 cloud decision-makers: 27% estimated waste on IaaS and PaaS, and spend over budget by 17%. Harness, FinOps in Focus 2025, estimated that 21% of cloud infrastructure spend would be wasted in 2025, equal to $44.5 billion. At about 75 pence to the dollar on 26 October 2026, that is roughly £33 billion worldwide, not a UK-only total.

The saving available on a single estate is the part of that waste which is idle, oversized, or expensive because of how it is built. Spare capacity for a real peak, and a path to roll back, should stay. The review is there to separate the two.

What Fransystems does with the bill

The engagement starts from the whole topology, on AWS, Azure, or both. That means the accounts, networks, compute, data, and the paths between them, not a sorted list of the ten largest lines. The output is the waste, the saving, and the improvement that keeps the platform operable.

What the review covers

  • Compute and platform services that are larger than the workload
  • Resources that are unused, forgotten, or left behind by an old environment
  • Architecture that is expensive because of its shape, not because of demand
  • Non-production that runs as if it were production
  • Commitment options, looked at after the waste is visible, not before

What you receive

  • A prioritised list of changes, not a dump of the billing console
  • The reliability consequence of each change, stated plainly
  • Which items are safe to do this week, and which need a design change
  • Enough detail for your own team to implement, or for Fransystems to build it in Terraform

Fransystems specialists recently did this for one company. Cloud costs came down by 32%, a saving of more than $140,000.

AWS and Azure, on the same engagement if needed

Cost work at Fransystems comes from running both clouds, not from a single vendor playbook. On AWS that includes multi-account estates, EC2, ECS, EKS, and Lambda. On Azure it includes App Services, WebJobs, Functions, and virtual machines. The same questions apply on both: what is idle, what is oversized, and what would a simpler design stop paying for every month.

Fransystems specialists do this alongside delivery, including while bringing platforms under Terraform. A saving that depends on a console click nobody will repeat is not the preference. Where the change belongs in code, it can be done that way. The Terraform page describes that work. The architecture page covers the cases where the bill is a symptom of the design.

This is not a race to the smallest possible invoice. Reliability stays in the recommendation. A cheaper design that pages someone every night is not a saving.
