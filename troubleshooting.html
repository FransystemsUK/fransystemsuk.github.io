A second set of eyes on the fault your customers feel

Twenty-seven years of IT and infrastructure, applied to the niggling issue the team has learned to live with. Fransystems finds why it keeps touching the customer, and stops it coming back.

The problem is rarely the alert

Your own engineers are close to the estate. That is also why the same fault survives: everyone has a story for it. A senior outsider has seen the pattern before, on other platforms, and is not attached to the workaround.

  • 27 years

    of IT and infrastructure. Third-line telecoms, Linux estates, Kubernetes, then production AWS and Azure. The fault is rarely new. The context is.

  • Second eyes

    A fresh read of the logs, the config, and the path the customer actually takes. Not another restart, and not a slide about what the monitoring might mean.

  • Customers

    The slow page, the failed overnight job, the retry the user has to do. If a customer can feel it, it is already a production incident, whether or not the dashboard is red.

What you hand over, and what you get back

Where the fault usually sits

  • AWS and Azure configuration that drifted from what people believe is deployed
  • Linux hosts, including estates large enough that one snowflake server matters
  • Docker, ECS microservices, and Kubernetes on EKS, AKS, or on-premises clusters
  • Azure App Services, WebJobs, Functions, and the way they are released
  • CI/CD that promoted something it should have stopped

What you leave with

  • The cause, separated from the symptoms that showed up in the alert
  • The change that restores service, made in a way the team can see
  • A runbook note or a permanent fix, so the same escalation does not return next week
  • A view on whether the design itself is what made the fault hard to see

Twenty-seven years of having to fix it for real

Fransystems specialists spent eight years in senior third-line support for enterprise telecommunications, on Linux session border controllers for accounts including HMRC and Barclays, and wrote more than 1,000 technical processes so the same escalation stopped returning. They then ran incidents across a contact-centre platform and a hosted telephony estate: AWS, Linux, Docker, Kubernetes, and a Linux estate of more than 200 servers. That is 27 years of IT and infrastructure, not a workshop exercise.

The recent work is the same habit on cloud platforms. EKS and ECS, Azure App Services and WebJobs, with CloudWatch, CloudTrail, Azure Monitor, the Elastic Stack, and OpenSearch as the evidence. The customer impact comes first. The permanent fix comes with it.

If the answer is a platform change rather than another restart, that continues as architecture and Terraform.
