A second set of eyes on the fault your customers feel

A fault a customer can feel, traced to the cause and stopped from coming back. Twenty-seven years of fixing live systems, applied to the problem the team has learned to live with.

The problem is rarely the alert

Your own people are close to the setup. That is also why the same fault survives: everyone has a story for it. A senior outsider has seen the pattern before, on other systems, and is not attached to the workaround.

  • 27 years

    of fixing IT. Phone systems, large Linux setups, then live Amazon and Microsoft. The fault is rarely new. The surroundings are.

  • Second eyes

    A fresh read of the logs, the settings, and the path the customer actually takes. Not another restart, and not a slide about what the monitoring might mean.

  • Customers

    The slow page, the job that failed overnight, the step the customer has to try again. If a customer can feel it, it is already a live problem, whether or not a dashboard is red.

What you hand over, and what you get back

Where the fault usually sits

  • Amazon or Microsoft settings that no longer match what people believe is running
  • Linux servers, including a large set where one machine was built by hand and behaves unlike the others
  • Applications packaged so they can be moved, on Amazon, on Microsoft, or on a company’s own servers
  • Microsoft’s hosted web apps, and the way they are released
  • A release process that sent a bad change live when it should have stopped

What you leave with

  • The cause, separated from the symptoms that showed up in the alert
  • The change that restores the service, made in a way the team can see
  • A short written note, or a lasting fix, so the same problem does not return next week
  • A view on whether the design itself is what made the fault hard to see

Twenty-seven years of having to fix it for real

Fransystems specialists spent eight years in senior support for business phone systems, on the servers that carry those calls for customers including HMRC and Barclays, and wrote more than 1,000 procedures so the same problem stopped coming back. They then handled live faults across a contact-centre product and a hosted phone system: Amazon, Linux, and the packaging used to run the applications, on more than 200 Linux servers. That is 27 years of fixing real systems, not a classroom exercise.

The recent work is the same habit on Amazon and Microsoft. The customer impact comes first. The lasting fix comes with it. The evidence is the logs those clouds already keep.

If the answer is a change to the design rather than another restart, that continues as architecture and Terraform.
