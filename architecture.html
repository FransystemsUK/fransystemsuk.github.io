Architectural advice for cloud upgrades

A practical route from the estate you have to one a team can operate. Advice that can be built, on AWS, on Azure, or across both.

The questions this is for

  • The account or subscription layout no longer matches how the business deploys
  • Networking has grown by exception and nobody wants to touch it
  • A workload might belong on ECS, on EKS, or on Azure PaaS, and the team wants a decision
  • A legacy platform and a newer one are both in production, and the path between them is unclear
  • An upgrade has stalled because the diagram and the estate disagree

What the advice contains

  • The target shape: landing zone, network, and where each workload should live
  • What to keep, what to replace, and in which order
  • The trade-off in cost, operability, and security for the options that were real
  • A sequence a Terraform change can follow, rather than a slide that ends the workshop

Drawn from platforms that were already live

The architectural work at Fransystems is landing zones, networking, containerised applications on ECS and EKS, and Azure PaaS. That sits on a longer record: a multi-account AWS organisation across UK and EU regions, a globally distributed Azure platform brought under a governed design, and, earlier, a legacy containerised contact-centre stack running beside a next-generation telephony platform.

Neil is an AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate and an Azure Administrator Associate. Those certificates are the vocabulary. The judgement comes from having to run the result, including on-premises Kubernetes and cloud platforms in the same career.

If the recommendation needs to become infrastructure, it continues on the Terraform page. If the current design is also the reason the bill or the incidents look the way they do, the cost and troubleshooting pages are the other way in. Security constraints belong in the design, not as a later pass: see security recommendations.
