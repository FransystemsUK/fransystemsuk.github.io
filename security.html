What a breach already costs

These are published figures, not a forecast for your company. They are what “we will tighten this later” is being weighed against.

    is the global average cost of a data breach. In the same study the UK average was $4.14 million, about £3.1 million.

  • £300 million

    is what Marks & Spencer estimated the 2025 attack would take from operating profit. Online clothing orders stopped. Stores fell back on paper.

  • 30%

    of UK micro businesses say cyber security is a low priority for senior managers. Only 21% have a formal incident response plan.

IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025, with Ponemon Institute: global average $4.44 million, United Kingdom $4.14 million. The sterling figure is that UK average at about 75 pence to the dollar. The same report found that 16% of breaches involved attackers using AI, most often for phishing and deepfake impersonation, and that 13% of organisations reported a breach of an AI model or application. Of those, 97% lacked proper AI access controls. Marks & Spencer, May 2025 market statement, and preliminary results for the year to 28 March 2026: direct incident costs £131.3 million, insurance proceeds £100 million, adjusted profit before tax down 23.8%. UK Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025/26: 43% of businesses, about 612,000, identified a breach or attack in the previous year. Among micro businesses, 42% did. Small businesses, 46%.

Many smaller firms leave security until later, because the product comes first. Can you wait? Almost a third of UK micro businesses already treat this as a low priority, and the attackers are not waiting for a quieter month. Marks & Spencer was not a small firm with no time, and the year still closed with a 23.8% fall in adjusted profit after the insurance money.

In the AI age, the test has changed

This is no longer a future risk. IBM found that 16% of the breaches in its 2025 study involved attackers using AI, most often to write fake emails and to impersonate people with fake voices or video. Thirteen percent of organisations reported a breach of an AI tool they were using, and 97% of that group had not properly limited who could use it. Those numbers will rise. The tools are already cheap, and the attacks that worked this year will be copied.

A check done by hand, once a year, against a list written before this, will not see the next attack. You either test the protection with the same kind of tools an attacker is already using, or you fail a test that is already running.

Fransystems advises on the whole setup: who can log in, how the network is joined, the hosting, the way a change is released, the data, and the AI tools the team has already started using. The advice is about your system, and it can be built into the written setup and the release path where that is what will actually hold.

What Fransystems looks at

The work is the protection on the system you are already running: who can change the live service, what the network allows, where the passwords and keys live, and whether a change can be shown afterwards. It is ordered so the first items close the doors that cannot wait for the next release.

What gets looked at

  • Who can log in, who can change the live service, and how that is supposed to work
  • What the network allows between accounts and the systems they talk to
  • Passwords and keys: where they live, who can read them, and how they reach an application
  • Tightening the servers, the packaged applications, and the cloud services you actually use
  • A change you can show evidence for. A rule nobody can prove was followed is a weak rule

How the recommendations are framed

  • Written for this setup, not a generic checklist with your name on the cover
  • Ordered by the open door they close, so the first items are worth doing
  • Written so they can be built into Terraform and into the way you release, where that is the right place
  • Honest about what is a settings change and what is a change to the design
