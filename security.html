What a breach already costs

These are published figures, not a forecast for your estate. They are what “we will harden it later” is being weighed against.

    is the global average cost of a data breach. In the same study the UK average was $4.14 million, about £3.1 million.

  • £300 million

    is what Marks & Spencer estimated the 2025 attack would take from operating profit. Online clothing orders stopped. Stores fell back on paper.

  • 30%

    of UK micro businesses say cyber security is a low priority for senior managers. Only 21% have a formal incident response plan.

IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025, with Ponemon Institute: global average $4.44 million, United Kingdom $4.14 million. The sterling figure is that UK average at about 75 pence to the dollar. The same report found that 16% of breaches involved attackers using AI, most often for phishing and deepfake impersonation, and that 13% of organisations reported a breach of an AI model or application. Of those, 97% lacked proper AI access controls. Marks & Spencer, May 2025 market statement, and preliminary results for the year to 28 March 2026: direct incident costs £131.3 million, insurance proceeds £100 million, adjusted profit before tax down 23.8%. UK Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025/26: 43% of businesses, about 612,000, identified a breach or attack in the previous year. Among micro businesses, 42% did. Small businesses, 46%.

Many smaller firms update the security posture later, because the product comes first. Can you wait? Almost a third of UK micro businesses already treat this as a low priority, and the attackers are not on the roadmap. Marks & Spencer was not a startup with no time, and the year still closed with a 23.8% fall in adjusted profit after the insurance money.

In the AI age, the test has changed

This is no longer a future risk. IBM found that 16% of the breaches in its 2025 study involved attackers using AI, most often to write phishing and to impersonate people with deepfakes. Thirteen percent of organisations reported a breach of an AI model or application, and 97% of that group lacked proper access controls on the AI. Those numbers will rise. The tools are already cheap, and the attacks that worked this year will be copied.

A posture tested by hand, once a year, against a checklist written before this, will not see the next one. You either adopt AI-based testing of the security posture, or you fail the test an attacker is already running.

What Fransystems looks at before later arrives

The work is the posture of the estate you are already running: who can change production, what the network allows, where the secrets live, and whether a change can be evidenced. It is ordered so the first items close the exposure that cannot wait for the next release.

What gets looked at

  • Identity and access: who can change production, and how that path is supposed to work
  • Network controls between accounts, subscriptions, and the systems they talk to
  • Secrets: where they live, who can read them, and how they arrive in a workload
  • Hardening of the hosts, containers, and platform services you actually use
  • Change that is audit-ready, because a control nobody can evidence is a weak control

How the recommendations are framed

  • Tied to this estate, not a generic checklist with your name on the cover
  • Ordered by the exposure they close, so the first items are worth doing
  • Written so they can be implemented in Terraform and in the pipeline, where that is the right place
  • Honest about what is a configuration change and what is a design change
