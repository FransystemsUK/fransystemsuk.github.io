Security recommendations

Controls that match how the platform is changed and run. IAM, network, secrets, hardening, and a change record that stands up to an audit.

What gets looked at

  • Identity and access: who can change production, and how that path is supposed to work
  • Network controls between accounts, subscriptions, and the systems they talk to
  • Secrets: where they live, who can read them, and how they arrive in a workload
  • Hardening of the hosts, containers, and platform services you actually use
  • Change that is audit-ready, because a control nobody can evidence is a weak control

How the recommendations are framed

  • Tied to this estate, not a generic checklist with your name on the cover
  • Ordered by the exposure they close, so the first items are worth doing
  • Written so they can be implemented in Terraform and in the pipeline, where that is the right place
  • Honest about what is a configuration change and what is a design change

Public-sector delivery and long-running operations

Neil is security-cleared and has delivered into Ministry of Defence environments, including two MoD environments inside a wider dual-cloud estate. Earlier, he operated Linux-based session border controllers for enterprise accounts including HMRC and Barclays, covering security policy and production change on global network interconnects.

That background is operational. The offer here is recommendations and, where you want it, the implementation of those controls. It is not a penetration test and it is not a compliance certificate. If the gap is architectural, it belongs with the architecture work. If the gap is an estate that cannot be changed safely because it is not in code, it belongs with Terraform.
